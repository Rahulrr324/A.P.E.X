1. EXACT DIAGNOSTIC METHOD
================================================================================

Binary:      stark/build-v24-release/diag_highres_frequency.exe
Source:      stark/tests/diag_highres_frequency.cpp
Wrapper:     power_scheme_control.exe run (session-scoped HP apply/restore)

Method:
  - PDH counter "\Processor Information(0,0)\% Processor Performance"
    collected by a dedicated background thread with a tight loop and QPC
    timestamps on every sample.
  - The collector loops tight ONLY while an exec is active (g_exec_active flag)
    and sleeps 2 ms between execs (addendum C1 / R1 design).
  - Collector thread pinned to LAST logical CPU (R1).
  - Measured thread (benchmark) pinned to CPU0 by
    HardwareAffinity::requestAndVerifyFirstCpu() (verified_single_cpu=1).
  - Each exec = TinyDenseModel::forward() with 10 warmup + 50 measured
    iterations, each iteration bracketed by std::chrono::steady_clock QPC
    (t0/t1/dur_us). IDENTICAL to the production harness config:
      warmup_count=10, measurement_count=50, thread_count=1, batch_size=1,
      sequence_length=0, precision=FP32, deterministic_mode_requested=true.
  - After each exec, the in-process collector samples whose QPC timestamp
    falls inside [first-iteration-t0, last-iteration-t1] are joined with the
    iteration bounds to compute EXEC-ONLY statistics:
      min / max / mean / median / spread_max_pct /
      n_plateaus / n_transitions / artifact_rate_pct /
      per_iteration mean_pct_perf + n_covering + Pearson r(dur,%perf) /
      downsampled trace (max 400 samples).
  - R2 artifact filter: usable iff 20.0 <= pct_perf <= 240.0.
  - P1: 2 s idle tight-loop probe (PDH response + noise floor).
  - P2: step-transition latency test (idle -> load on CPU0, probe until %perf
       moves >5% from idle mean; then 1 s loaded collection).
  - P3: 10 production execs: B6 x6, B9 x2, B7 x2 (gap / no-gap each),
       with 30 s idle gaps between B6 groups and before B9/B7.
  - The production benchmark PATH is UNCHANGED. No STARKP1 records written.
    No record store touched.

Exec-window timing (EXACT, per spec):
  For every exec, t_start_utc / t_end_utc are recorded, and the per-iteration
  QPC brackets [t0_us .. t1_us] are written verbatim. The exec-span is
  [min(t0), max(t1)] across the 50 iterations. These are in the JSON under
  exec_t0_us, exec_t1_us, exec_span_us, and the iterations[] array.

Before/during/after capture:
  - BEFORE exec: collector samples the PDH baseline during the preceding idle
    window (and during the 2 ms sleep between execs).
  - DURING exec: collector tight-loops on the last logical CPU; any sample
    whose t_us is inside the exec QPC span is tagged as in-exec.
  - AFTER exec: collector resumes tight collection during the next idle gap,
    capturing the post-exec frequency state.

ROOT CAUSE of the two zero-coverage execs (same as v3 but NOW CONFIRMED ON A
FRESH RUN):
  - Process affinity = {CPU0} (verified_single_cpu=1) on BOTH this run and v3.
  - The collector is pinned to the "last logical CPU" per R1. Under a
    process-wide mask of {CPU0}, the last logical CPU is CPU0 itself. So the
    collector runs on CPU0 — the same CPU as the measured thread.
  - B6 and B9 are pure compute (matrix multiply, tight FP32 loops). They hold
    CPU0 at ~100% for the entire 6.5-7 ms exec. A tight PDH loop on the same
    CPU gets starved and accumulates zero samples whose timestamps fall inside
    the exec QPC span. (Samples may still be collected by the thread between
    iterations, but those miss the exec-span window.)
  - Some B6 execs (0,1,3,4,5) DID get in-exec samples, meaning the scheduler
    gave the collector brief CPU windows (e.g. during iteration boundaries or
    OS scheduling ticks), but two execs got zero. This is non-deterministic
    scheduler behavior, not a deterministic failure — which is itself why a
    fixed pinning strategy (collector on a genuinely separate logical CPU) is
    the right fix rather than hoping the scheduler cooperates.
  - B9 exec7 (no-gap, immediately after exec6) got zero coverage even though
    exec6 had 6,427 samples. The no-gap exec starts sooner (no 30s idle) and
    the scheduler had less opportunity to give the collector a window.

CORROBORATING EVIDENCE THAT B6/B9 FREQUENCY WAS STABLE (indirect):
  - All B6 execs with coverage show median 209.333 (4 of 5), one shows 211.333
    (exec0). The two levels are the discrete PDH representation plateaus.
  - B9 exec6 median = 209.000 (same plateau family).
  - B6 within-exec TIMING is stable: first25 vs last25 medians differ by
    <2% (iteration_drift_pct), AND the per-exec median_us varies only
    131-152 us (timing spread 13.8%). If genuine frequency varied 5-9% within

  - B9 exec6 median_us=136.5 vs exec7 median_us=134.0 (timing spread 1.8%).

  - B7 (which HAS dense coverage) shows stable 209.333 median across both

This indirect evidence is STRONG but not direct for B6/B9. The 2 zero-coverage

6. CLASSIFICATION: C (MIXED/INDETERMINATE)
================================================================================

Formal basis (predeclared rule, same as v3):
  - PDH counter adequate: YES (28 us step response, 5 us cadence, 359,707
    samples in 2 s). The counter is NOT the bottleneck.
  - Coverage floor: >=10 usable in-exec samples per exec.
  - Coverage result: 2 of 10 execs (B6 exec2, B9 exec7) have 0 usable samples.
  - Per predeclared rule: if ANY exec has <10 usable samples, classify C.
  - Classification = C.

Evidence leaning toward B (artifact — sampling-window / PDH noise):
  (a) The PDH counter is NOISY: 94.2% of consecutive idle samples differ.
      Plateau durations are very short. Raw per-sample spread is 47-91% even
      inside execs where frequency is actually stable (median constant). The
      6.8974% exec-window spread is in the same noise regime as the 5-91%
      spreads seen inside execs — i.e. it is consistent with being noise.
  (b) The exec-only MEDIANS are stable at discrete levels (209.000/209.333/
      211.333) and do NOT drift within execs. The per-iteration mean %perf for
      B7 is stable to stdev ~2.5-3.2%. If genuine frequency ramps of the size
      implied by 6.8974% existed, the median would move within execs or between
      gap/no-gap execs; it does not.
  (c) B6/B9 TIMING is stable (within-exec drift <2%, between-exec timing spread
      1.8-13.8%), which is consistent with stable frequency (a real 5-9%
      frequency swing would show as a proportional timing swing, which is
      absent).
  (d) The 6.8974% figure comes from the exec-WINDOW metric (before/after
      snapshots), which captures the frequency state at two instants bracketing
      the exec. The v3 and v4 data show the before-snapshot %perf values differ
      sharply from the exec-internal median — proving the exec-window metric
      captures between-exec frequency state, not within-exec variation.

Evidence preventing a formal B classification:
  (e) Two B6/B9 execs have ZERO in-exec frequency samples. We cannot directly
      prove those specific execs were frequency-stable.
  (f) B7 is a different benchmark (file I/O + deserialize) from B6/B9 (matrix
      multiply). While B7's stable frequency strongly suggests the scheme holds
      frequency stable generally, the predeclared rule does not allow
      extrapolating B7's stability to B6/B9 formally.

Why this is NOT classification A:
  - No within-exec frequency ramp is evidenced. The median is constant within
    every exec that has coverage. The high spread_max values (47-91%) are PDH
    noise (confirmed by P1: 94.2% sample-to-sample change at idle, and by the
    stable medians/means). The artifact_rate is 2-9%, and the "transitions"
    detected are sub-percent fluctuations, not real steps.
  - B6/B9 within-exec timing drift is <2%, inconsistent with a genuine
    multi-percent frequency ramp.

Conclusion on the 6.8974% residual:
  The 6.8974% exec-window spread is a SAMPLING-WINDOW artifact: it is the
  (max-min)/max of two PDH snapshot values taken before and after each exec.
  Those snapshots capture the instantaneous %perf at the snapshot instants,
  which differs from the exec-internal stable level (median 209.333). The
  difference between the before-snapshot values across execs (caused by
  real between-exec frequency changes under the OS power scheduler, even in HP
  mode) is what inflates the exec-window spread to 6.8974%.

Therefore the residual is best characterized as classification C, with the
weight of evidence leaning toward B (artifact) — but the two zero-coverage
B6/B9 execs prevent a formal B classification under the predeclared rule.

7. WHETHER ANOTHER CONTROL IS JUSTIFIED
================================================================================

NOT YET — the correct next step is to FIX THE DIAGNOSTIC, not add another
power-state control.

Reasoning:
  - Under the EXISTING High Performance scheme, the exec-only data shows that
    within-exec frequency IS stable where we can measure it (B7 medians
    identical across gap/no-gap; B6/B9 medians at the same discrete stable
    levels; B6/B9 timing stable).
  - The 6.8974% exec-window spread is NOT within-execution variation. It is
    the exec-window snapshot metric capturing between-exec frequency state plus
    PDH noise. A tighter power scheme would not fix a measurement artifact.
  - Adding another control now would target a problem (within-exec frequency
    variation) that the evidence shows does NOT exist. That would be premature.

The remaining uncertainty is narrow and specific: 2 of 10 B6/B9 execs have
zero in-exec frequency samples because the collector starves on CPU0. This is
a diagnostic coverage gap, not a power-scheme gap.

Recommended diagnostic fix (lowest risk first):
  (a) Re-run the SAME diag_highres_frequency.exe with an EXPANDED process-wide
      affinity of at least 2 logical CPUs, while keeping the MEASURED thread
      pinned to CPU0 (the benchmark thread affinity is set inside
      HardwareAffinity::requestAndVerifyFirstCpu() / the harness; the
      diagnostic's PROCESS affinity is what must be widened so the collector's
      "last logical CPU" is a genuinely different CPU from CPU0). This gives
      the collector a free CPU and should yield dense in-exec samples for ALL
      B6/B9 execs. This changes only the DIAGNOSTIC's process affinity, not the
      production benchmark's thread affinity or semantics.
  (b) If (a) is not acceptable, add PDH sampling on the main thread immediately
      after each iteration's t1 (one sample per iteration, ~130-150 us spacing
      for B6/B9, 50 samples/exec). Coarser than the tight-loop collector but
      far finer than 100 ms, and immune to the starvation problem because no
      second thread is involved.

With (a) or (b) giving direct B6/B9 exec-only frequency data, the classification
can be formally re-evaluated. If the direct data confirms stable frequency
(as B7 and the timing data strongly suggest), the 5% exec-window gate itself
should be re-examined: it is measuring between-exec frequency state plus noise,
not within-exec stability, and may be the wrong metric for the stability
property the campaign actually needs.
